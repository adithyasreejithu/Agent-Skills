## The question
If you put $10,000 into AMD a year ago you would have about $35,000; into Micron, almost $60,000 (a 10-bagger in two years). Which of the two is the better buy now? His answer: keep buying Micron, wait on AMD.

## Why both stocks jumped
- **AI agents change the chip mix:** a chatbot is mostly GPU work, but agents loop through many steps where tool calls, code and database lookups run on CPUs. GPU-to-CPU ratios in AI data centers are shrinking from 8:1 toward 4:1 and 2:1, and in some cases there may be more CPUs than GPUs.
- **CPUs are short:** AMD's CEO says the server CPU supply chain has been tight all year. Intel says it can serve about half its customers. AMD popped almost 10% in a day and hit a $1 trillion market cap.
- **Memory is shorter:** Micron expects tightness beyond 2027, Samsung through 2028. Micron can meet only about half to two-thirds of major customers' demand. DRAM prices rose more than 60% last spring. Every processor needs memory (an Nvidia Vera Rubin rack carries about 21 TB of GPU memory and up to 54 TB on the CPU side), and AI agents also push data out of GPU memory (KV cache) into DRAM and SSDs.
- **Memory pays whoever wins:** Micron's data center segment brought in $34B last quarter, almost three times AMD's and Intel's data center revenue combined.

| Biggest risk | Warrants give OpenAI and Meta up to 160M shares each at 1 cent, so up to about 16% dilution | Memory price rises slowing (+60% in spring, under 20% in summer), heavy factory spending ($27B last year, about $25B in six months) |

- **AMD's case:** server CPU share about 46% of x86 revenue, but it also faces ARM chips and Nvidia's custom Vera CPUs, and each share point gets harder. To justify its price at Nvidia and Broadcom's 17x to 18x forward earnings, AMD would need about $35 a share next year versus Wall Street's $15.60. At that multiple the stock would be under $250.
- **Micron's case:** a third or more of revenue is locked into 26 long-term contracts (about $32B of commitments) through 2030, with price floors, and most high-bandwidth memory is sold out through 2027. At 17x to 18x earnings the stock would be over $2,700, so today's price assumes earnings fall about 60% and stay there. Free cash flow was $33B last quarter with $68B more cash than debt, and buybacks are ramping.
- **The honest risk:** memory is a brutal boom-and-bust industry. Micron looks cheapest at its peak (4.5x in January 2018, then down 27%; 6.5x in mid-2022, then down another 35%), and the stock tends to peak before earnings do. New fabs (Idaho, Japan) start between mid-2027 and late 2028, and Samsung's HBM share rose from 21% to 33% in a quarter while Micron's fell from 21% to 18%.

## His call
Keep buying Micron. Stop if DRAM contract prices flatten or fall while spending keeps rising. Buy AMD only after a big pullback or if earnings grow enough to justify the multiple.
