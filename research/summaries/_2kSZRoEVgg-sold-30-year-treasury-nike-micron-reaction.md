## 1. "Why aren't more people buying 30-year Treasuries?"
He reacts to an X post: a $1M 30-year Treasury at 5.6% pays $56,000 a year for doing nothing.
- **Inflation and tax:** $1M in 30 years is worth about $460,000 in today's dollars (a modest inflation assumption). $56,000 a year is about $42,000 after tax, which would be about $20,000 in today's dollars in 30 years, so he calls it a poor way to live, especially in a big city.
- **The alternative:** the S&P 500 has compounded about 10% a year over 30 years (about 13% for the Nasdaq) through three major crashes. $1M at 10% grows to about $17M in 30 years. Taking the Treasury-sized $56K a year for the first decade and raising withdrawals later would still leave far more. A strong stock picker might do 15% to 20%.
- His own view: people should live on what they choose, but most goals (house, health care, schools, giving) need more than $40K to $50K a year. His personal spending is about $40K to $50K a month.

## 3. Reaction: Jeremy Siegel on CNBC (bond yields)
- The 30-year inflation-protected yield reached about 3.35%, a level not seen in 20 to 30 years. A 10% stock market still gives about 5% after inflation, but the gap is shrinking.
- Tech holds up because its margins are 50% to 70%, while the "real world" at 7% to 10% margins is squeezed by higher rates. This stalled the spring rotation.
- Fed chair Warsh faces a hard meeting six days before the midterms. Siegel expects two more increases this year, and said the economy can take higher short-term rates.
- **His takeaway (two ways to crash the market):** (a) rates cut to near zero in a deep recession, or (b) Treasury yields climbing toward 7% or 8%, where people stop buying stocks. He thinks 5.2% to 5.6% is far from that, and says many stocks (Nike is at a 2013 price) are generational buying opportunities.
